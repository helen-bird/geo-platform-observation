Who does AI recommend, and whose content does it cite?

A small repeated-query comparison of ChatGPT Search and Grok, covering learning resources, empirical research and practical GEO tutorials.

  • Q3 recommendations shared only 2 of 20 unique entities/resources. The 10% overlap pools two answers per platform. The shared resources were GEO research (Aggarwal et al.) and The GEO Community. The lists mix papers, communities, organizations and individual creators; this is not a comparison of 20 creators.
  • Observed own-content citations accompanied 47/48 ChatGPT Search and 39/76 Grok recommendation records. These 97.9% and 51.3% figures cover coded entity-by-answer records and captured citations only. They do not measure answer accuracy or inherent platform citation preferences.
  • Two repeats produced different recommendation sets, with important condition differences. Neither platform had the greater overlap on every question. ChatGPT Q2 spans dates; Q3 also changes reasoning effort. Account memory was observed, and Grok’s backend conditions were undisclosed. These are descriptive overlaps, not estimates of stable consistency.
3 original questions × 2 platforms × 2 repeats = 12 selected complete answers. The archive also retains 10 other submissions and 37 captures; captures are not extra prompts. There was no optimization intervention, baseline or control group, so these observations cannot establish GEO effectiveness.

Resource types and a bounded source review

The recommendations include several resource types. A declared post-hoc classification separates individual creators, papers/studies, communities, organizations and tools rather than treating them all as products or creators.

A purposive audit checks five Q3 recommendations per platform for existence, original experiments, reported methods/sample sizes and fit for independent builders. All ten selected pages were readable at the check.

Relevance and original evidence are different checks. The selected Seer page interprets an academic study rather than establishing a Seer-run experiment. The selected GEO Lab manual describes a Perplexity experiment but reports inconsistent sample denominators and significance wording. These are page-specific qualifications, not judgments of either entity’s entire work.

This assistant audit is supplementary, not independent human validation or a representative quality ranking. An accessible page with a stated method does not verify all claims or reproduce the study. Original names, recommendation labels and the 2/20 overlap remain unchanged.

What this cannot show

  • No causal GEO conclusion: there is no content intervention, pre-optimization baseline, control group, predefined brand panel or conversion measure.
  • No inherent platform ranking: dates, reasoning effort, account context, source capture and undisclosed backend conditions limit the comparison.
  • No finding that an absent brand has poor GEO: answers depend on the sampled questions and conditions, and the mixed recommendation inventories are not a brand census.
The assistant coded the answers; no independent human double-coding review was completed. The separate source spot-check does not remove coding subjectivity or validate every external experiment. Multiple tutorials citing the same study are not independent replications.
