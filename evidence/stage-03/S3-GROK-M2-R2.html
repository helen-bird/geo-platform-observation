Original prompt

As a bootstrapped SaaS builder, what's a free, repeatable workflow for checking whether my product appears in ChatGPT and Grok recommendations?

The agreed prompt is reproduced exactly. Any recorded difference in the actual submitted text or search UI is retained in the raw record and full appendix.

Original answer

A free, repeatable workflow is a fixed set of buyer-intent prompts + manual sampling in fresh sessions + a simple spreadsheet. This is the standard DIY approach used by bootstrapped founders and small teams for ChatGPT and Grok (and other engines). Paid trackers exist, but they are unnecessary for a baseline and trend view.

1. Build a fixed prompt set (do this once)

Create 8–15 prompts that real buyers would type before they know your brand name. Freeze the exact wording so you can compare over time.

Core types:

Category discovery: “What’s the best [your category] for [specific use case / company size / audience]?”
Alternatives: “Best alternatives to [leading competitor]”
Comparisons: “[Competitor A] vs [Competitor B] for [use case]”
Problem / job-to-be-done: “How do I [the outcome your product delivers] without [common pain]?”

Pull language from customer calls, support tickets, Google’s “People also ask,” Reddit, or your own Search Console queries. Avoid pure branded questions (“Is [your product] good?”) for discovery checks—they inflate results. Keep branded prompts only for accuracy checks (pricing, features, downsides).

2. Run the checks cleanly

ChatGPT (chatgpt.com):

Use a Temporary Chat and choose the unpersonalized option (or log out / use a private window). This reduces memory and personalization bias.
Prefer sessions with web search enabled when available.
Run each prompt 2–3 times in fresh chats.

Grok (grok.com or via X):

Start a fresh conversation each time.
Grok draws heavily from X posts in addition to the web, so its recommendations can differ from ChatGPT.
Same 2–3 runs per prompt.

For every answer, note:

Did your product appear? (Y/N)
Position / order if it was in a list
Competitors named
Sources cited (especially useful)
Brief framing (how it described you or why it preferred someone else)
3. Log it in a spreadsheet

One row per run. Columns that matter:

| Date | Engine | Prompt | Mentioned (Y/N) | Position | Competitors | Citations | Notes |

At the end of each cycle, calculate simple metrics:

Mention rate (how often you appear on unbranded prompts)
Share of voice vs the 2–3 competitors that keep showing up
Citation rate (how often your site or a trusted third-party page about you is linked)

A Google Sheet or Notion table is enough. No special tools required.

4. Cadence
Run a full baseline now.
Repeat monthly (or every 2 weeks on your 5–6 highest-priority prompts).
Extra spot-checks after major content launches, new reviews (G2/Capterra/etc.), or big X activity.

Time cost is typically 20–40 minutes per cycle once the prompts are set. Answers are non-deterministic—the same prompt can produce different shortlists across sessions, models, and dates—so the trend across multiple runs matters more than any single screenshot.

Practical notes for bootstrapped SaaS
Free one-shot checkers (Ahrefs Brand Radar free preview, various “AI visibility” free tools) can give a quick extra data point, but they don’t replace your controlled, repeated prompt set.
Ensure your site is crawlable (don’t block major AI crawlers in robots.txt).
For Grok specifically, an authentic presence on X helps because of its heavier weighting of X content.
This measures whether you appear in recommendations. Improving it usually means earning consistent third-party coverage (listicles, reviews, comparisons) plus clear, accurate pages on your own site—not paid placements (OpenAI has stated product results are not ads).

This workflow is free, fully under your control, and gives a clear before/after view of whether ChatGPT and Grok are recommending your product. Start with the prompt list and a spreadsheet this week; the rest is just consistent sampling.
