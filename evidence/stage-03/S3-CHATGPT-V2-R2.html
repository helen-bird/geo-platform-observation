Original prompt

As a bootstrapped SaaS builder, what checks should I perform before trusting reported ChatGPT and Grok visibility scores for my product?

The agreed prompt is reproduced exactly. Any recorded difference in the actual submitted text or search UI is retained in the raw record and full appendix.

Original answer

Treat any “AI visibility score” as a **vendor-defined estimate**, not an official ChatGPT or Grok metric. Before paying for or acting on it, perform these checks:

### 1. Demand an exact metric definition

Determine whether “visibility” means:

- Your product name appears anywhere
- Your website is cited
- You are included in a recommendation list
- You rank in the top three
- The answer describes you accurately and positively
- Your product appears across a percentage of tested prompts

Ask for the actual numerator, denominator, weighting formula, and treatment of “not sure” or failed responses. A single composite score can otherwise hide meaningful differences between **mention, citation, recommendation, and preference**.

### 2. Inspect the prompt set

Request the complete prompts—not merely prompt categories. Check that they:

- Reflect questions real prospects ask
- Include non-branded, problem-aware queries
- Cover alternatives, comparisons, and use cases
- Represent your target geography and customer segment
- Aren’t artificially favorable to your product
- Include both head terms and long-tail prompts

Also check who selected the prompts. A vendor-generated benchmark may measure visibility for its imagined market rather than yours.

### 3. Confirm the exact test environment

For every run, require:

- Platform: consumer app or API
- Model/version
- Search-enabled versus model-only mode
- Standard, research, reasoning, or other mode
- Date, time, country, language, and device context
- Fresh chat versus an existing conversation
- Memory, custom instructions, and personalization status
- Logged-in versus logged-out account

This matters because ChatGPT can rewrite prompts into multiple search queries and may use approximate location or memory; Grok may decide whether to search the broader web, X, or neither. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search))

### 4. Require repeated samples

One run per prompt is not trustworthy. Ask for:

- At least 5–10 independent runs per important prompt
- New conversations for each run
- Mention and citation frequency, not just a binary result
- Confidence intervals or raw counts
- Variance across days

For example, “mentioned in 7 of 10 runs” is substantially more useful than an opaque score of 72.

### 5. Review the raw evidence

The report should let you inspect:

- Full prompt
- Full response
- Timestamp
- Citations and source URLs
- Model and mode
- Screenshots or, preferably, machine-readable run logs

Click citations and verify that they actually support the claims. OpenAI explicitly warns that search results and citations can be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search))

For Grok API-based testing, ask whether the provider retained the actual web/X search events and returned sources—not merely links generated in the answer. xAI’s documentation supports exposing search sources for this kind of verification. ([docs.x.ai](https://docs.x.ai/cookbook/examples/x-post-fact-check))

### 6. Separate model knowledge from live-search visibility

A product can appear because:

1. It exists in model training data,
2. The assistant found its website,
3. The assistant found a third-party article,
4. It appeared in an X post,
5. The prompt effectively supplied the answer.

These are different acquisition channels and require different actions. Search visibility should be reported separately from non-search recall.

### 7. Audit source quality

Check what sources are driving your visibility:

- Your own site
- Review platforms
- Software directories
- Affiliates
- Reddit, forums, or X
- Editorial comparisons
- Outdated pages
- Competitor-authored content

A high score built on inaccurate listicles or old pricing pages may be a liability. Also verify that your site is technically accessible: ChatGPT search eligibility depends partly on permitting OAI-SearchBot, although inclusion and placement are not guaranteed. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search))

### 8. Check competitor normalization

Ask:

- Which competitors were included—and why?
- Is the score absolute or relative?
- Does adding a competitor change your score?
- Are large incumbents and niche alternatives weighted equally?
- Are generic options such as spreadsheets or “build it yourself” included?

A “20% share of voice” is meaningless without the complete comparison set.

### 9. Test factual accuracy, not merely presence

Manually grade answers for:

- Correct positioning
- Current features and pricing
- Appropriate customer fit
- Correct category
- Accurate pros and cons
- Confusion with similarly named companies
- Broken or irrelevant citations

A confident but inaccurate recommendation should count negatively, not as successful visibility.

### 10. Reproduce a sample independently

Before trusting the dashboard:

1. Select 20–30 high-intent prompts.
2. Run them yourself in fresh ChatGPT and Grok conversations.
3. Record mentions, citations, rank, accuracy, and sentiment.
4. Repeat on several days.
5. Compare your results with the vendor’s raw data.

You need not reproduce the precise composite score. You should be able to reproduce its **direction and major conclusions**.

### 11. Look for business correlation

Compare reported visibility changes with:

- `utm_source=chatgpt.com` referrals
- Grok/X referrals where identifiable
- Branded-search growth
- Signup surveys asking “How did you hear about us?”
- Demo or signup volume from AI-assistant users
- Assisted conversions, not just last-click conversions

Visibility without qualified traffic, accurate recommendations, or conversions is primarily a branding indicator—not an ROI metric. ChatGPT adds referral tracking parameters to relevant outbound links, which can help with validation. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq%23%3A~%3Atext%3DChatGPT%20automatically%20includes%20the%20UTM%20parameter%20utm_source%3Dchatgpt.com%20in%20referral%20URLs%2C%20enabling%20clear%20tracking%20and%20analysis%20of%20inbound%20traffic%20from%20ChatGPT%20search%20results))

### Minimum evidence I’d accept

For a bootstrapped SaaS, I would not pay much for a score unless the provider supplies:

- Complete prompt list
- Metric formula
- Model/mode/location details
- Multiple runs per prompt
- Raw responses and citations
- Historical timestamps
- Separate ChatGPT and Grok results
- Mention, citation, rank, accuracy, and sentiment breakdowns
- Exportable data
- Results you can approximately reproduce

**Practical rule:** Use the score to identify prompts and source gaps, not as a KPI by itself. Track a small first-party benchmark—perhaps 25–50 commercially important prompts—alongside referral and conversion data. That is usually more actionable for a bootstrapped company than a broad proprietary “visibility” number.
