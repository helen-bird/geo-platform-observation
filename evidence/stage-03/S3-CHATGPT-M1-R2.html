Original prompt

I'm a solo SaaS founder with no GEO budget. How can I reliably measure whether ChatGPT and Grok recommend my product to potential buyers?

The agreed prompt is reproduced exactly. Any recorded difference in the actual submitted text or search UI is retained in the raw record and full appendix.

Original answer

You need to measure **two different things**:

1. **AI visibility:** Does your product appear in relevant ChatGPT/Grok answers?
2. **Business impact:** Are those answers producing visits, signups, or sales?

You can measure both manually for free, but don’t treat a few prompts from your own account as representative. Both services can personalize answers, and both may search the web depending on the query. ChatGPT can use memories, location, and rewritten search queries; Grok can use X activity and interests for personalization. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-in-chatgpt))

## 1. Build a fixed “buyer prompt panel”

Create 15–30 prompts reflecting how buyers actually describe their problem—not prompts containing your brand.

Cover four categories:

| Intent | Example |
|---|---|
| Category | “What are the best invoicing tools for solo consultants?” |
| Problem | “How can I automate overdue invoice follow-ups?” |
| Requirements | “Recommend an invoicing SaaS under $20/month with Stripe integration.” |
| Alternatives | “What are simpler alternatives to QuickBooks for freelancers?” |

Include buyer context such as company size, geography, budget, required integrations, and technical level.

Your primary metric should be **unaided recommendation**: whether your product appears when you did not mention it.

Separately test **aided knowledge** with prompts such as:

- “What is `[Product]`?”
- “Compare `[Product]` with `[Competitor]`.”
- “Who should and shouldn’t use `[Product]`?”

Aided tests diagnose what the model knows; they do not measure discovery.

## 2. Run a controlled audit

For each prompt:

- Start a fresh conversation.
- Use identical wording on both platforms.
- Don’t correct or guide the answer.
- Save the complete response and cited sources.
- Repeat each prompt three times on different days.
- Record date, model/mode, whether web search occurred, location, and personalization state.

For ChatGPT, use **Temporary Chat with personalization disabled** where available. Also test two separate tracks:

- **Default behavior**
- **Explicit web search:** append “Search the web and cite current sources.”

This distinction matters because answers without search rely more heavily on learned model knowledge, while searched answers draw from current web sources. OpenAI also cautions that citations and search results can be incomplete or incorrect, so inspect the actual cited pages. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt))

For Grok, disable personalization where possible or use a clean testing account. Grok may search both public X posts and the broader web, and its answers can be personalized using account activity and interests. ([help.x.com](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/about-grok))

## 3. Score every answer consistently

Use a simple 0–3 score:

- **0:** Not mentioned
- **1:** Mentioned, but not recommended
- **2:** Positively recommended or shortlisted
- **3:** Top recommendation

Add separate columns for:

- Position in the list
- Description accurate? Yes/no
- Correct target customer? Yes/no
- Website linked or cited? Yes/no
- Third-party source cited?
- Competitors recommended
- Any serious factual error

Then calculate:

```text
Mention rate = Runs mentioning you / Total runs

Recommendation rate = Runs scoring 2 or 3 / Total runs

Top-pick rate = Runs scoring 3 / Total runs

Citation rate = Runs citing your site / Searched runs

Consistency rate = Prompts where you appear in at least 2 of 3 runs / Total prompts
```

Also calculate **competitor share of voice**:

```text
Your recommendation appearances /
All recommendation appearances across you and tracked competitors
```

The consistency metric is especially important. Appearing once in nine runs is materially different from appearing eight times, even if someone posts the one successful screenshot.

## 4. Use this spreadsheet structure

One row per run:

```text

```

Maintain a separate prompt sheet with:

```text
Prompt ID
Intent category
Buyer persona
Buying stage
Business importance: 1–5
```

Weight your overall score by business importance. A recommendation for a high-converting “alternative to X” query matters more than one for a broad informational query.

## 5. Track actual AI-generated pipeline

Visibility testing tells you what *can* happen. Attribution tells you whether buyers are experiencing it.

### In analytics and server logs

Look for referral traffic from domains associated with ChatGPT and Grok. However, treat referrer data as a lower bound: apps, redirects, and privacy controls may prevent the original source from being passed.

Track:

- AI-referred sessions
- Signup rate
- Trial-to-paid rate
- Revenue
- Landing pages receiving AI traffic

### On signup

Add a required or optional question:

> How did you first hear about us?

Options:

- ChatGPT
- Grok
- Another AI assistant
- Google/search engine
- Social media
- Recommendation
- Other

If they select an AI assistant, ask:

> What were you asking it?

That answer is often more valuable than analytics because it gives you the real buyer prompt.

### During customer conversations

Ask:

> What other products did the AI recommend, and why did you choose us?

This reveals whether you were merely mentioned or actually framed as the best fit.

## 6. Use a sustainable solo-founder cadence

A reasonable free process:

### Weekly — 20 minutes

- Test your five highest-intent prompts once on each platform.
- Review new AI-attributed signups.
- Investigate any incorrect descriptions.

### Monthly — 60–90 minutes

- Run the full 15–30 prompt panel.
- Repeat important prompts three times.
- Calculate mention, recommendation, top-pick, citation, and consistency rates.
- Compare against the previous month.

Avoid testing every day and reacting to individual changes. Models, search results, source selection, and wording vary; evaluate rolling monthly trends instead.

## Your minimal dashboard

Track only these six numbers:

1. High-intent recommendation rate
2. Top-three placement rate
3. Consistency rate
4. Share of voice versus three competitors
5. AI-attributed signups
6. AI-attributed revenue

Your most defensible statement will then be something like:

> “Across 20 fixed buyer prompts and 120 controlled runs this month, we were recommended in 18% of ChatGPT answers and 11% of Grok answers. Five signups and one paid conversion self-attributed to those assistants.”

That is far more meaningful than claiming you “rank in ChatGPT.” There is no single universal ranking—only recommendation rates under documented prompts, conditions, and dates.
