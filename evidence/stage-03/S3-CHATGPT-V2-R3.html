Original prompt

As a bootstrapped SaaS builder, what checks should I perform before trusting reported ChatGPT and Grok visibility scores for my product?

The agreed prompt is reproduced exactly. Any recorded difference in the actual submitted text or search UI is retained in the raw record and full appendix.

Original answer

Treat any “AI visibility score” as a **vendor-defined benchmark—not an objective market metric**. Before trusting it, perform these checks:

## 1. Get the precise score definition

Ask what actually counts:

- A product-name mention?
- Inclusion in a recommendation list?
- A top-three position?
- A favorable recommendation?
- A citation or link to your domain?
- A citation to a third party that mentions you?
- How are rankings, sentiment, and citations weighted?

Request the formula, denominator, weighting, and one worked example. A score such as “42% visibility” is meaningless unless you know whether that means 42% of prompts, runs, weighted impressions, or something else.

## 2. Inspect the prompt set

Verify that you can export every tested prompt. Check for:

- **Representative intent:** category discovery, alternatives, comparisons, use cases, pricing, implementation and problem-oriented queries.
- **Branded separation:** “Best alternatives to MyProduct” should not inflate your non-branded discovery score.
- **Real buyer language:** prompts should resemble customer questions, not awkward SEO keyword templates.
- **Relevant segmentation:** geography, language, company size, industry and buyer persona.
- **No cherry-picking:** failed prompts and zero-mention prompts must remain in the denominator.

For a small SaaS, manually label the 20–50 prompts that actually matter commercially rather than relying on hundreds of generic prompts.

## 3. Confirm the exact test environment

Require the report to identify:

- Product: ChatGPT UI, Grok UI or API
- Model/version
- Search enabled or disabled
- Search, research, shopping or standard-chat mode
- Account tier and logged-in state
- Country/location
- Memory, personalization and custom-instruction settings
- Test date and harness version

This matters because ChatGPT may automatically search, rewrite queries, and use location or memory; Grok can decide whether to search the web or X and may personalize results using X activity. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8590148-memory-in-chatgpt))

Do not accept an API test as a direct proxy for what customers see in the consumer interface unless the vendor demonstrates that the configurations match.

## 4. Demand repeated runs and uncertainty

A single answer to a single prompt is not a reliable measurement. Ask for:

- Number of repetitions per prompt
- Mention rate across runs
- Mean score plus spread or confidence interval
- Results by day, not just a lifetime aggregate
- Consistent treatment of timeouts, refusals and truncated responses

xAI’s own evaluation guidance notes that reasoning-model outputs are nondeterministic, recommends multiple runs, and advises recording model, settings, date and harness version. ([docs.x.ai](https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/text/best-practices))

A practical bootstrap test is **30–50 important prompts × 3 runs on two different days**. Large score changes that disappear under this test are probably noise.

## 5. Review raw evidence

You should be able to inspect or export:

- Exact prompt
- Full answer
- Timestamp
- Model and mode
- Citations and URLs
- Your detected mention
- Competitor mentions
- The scoring decision

Randomly audit at least 10–20% of results. If a dashboard supplies only a composite score or cropped answer snippets, don’t trust it.

## 6. Separate mention, recommendation and citation

Track these independently:

1. **Awareness:** the model recognizes your product.
2. **Mention:** it includes you in an answer.
3. **Prominence:** your position in the list.
4. **Recommendation:** it positively presents you as suitable.
5. **Accuracy:** pricing, features and positioning are correct.
6. **Citation:** it links to evidence.
7. **Owned citation:** the link goes to your site.
8. **Conversion potential:** the answer gives a user a reason to visit.

A high mention score with inaccurate positioning or no links may be much less valuable than a lower score containing strong, correctly sourced recommendations.

## 7. Validate every citation

Check whether:

- The cited page actually supports the claim.
- The URL is live, canonical and current.
- The model is citing your website versus a directory or outdated review.
- A source merely contains your name or genuinely recommends the product.
- Citations were returned by the platform rather than invented by the monitoring vendor.

OpenAI explicitly warns that search results and citations can be incomplete, outdated or incorrect and recommends opening the source to verify support. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8313428))

## 8. Check entity resolution

Look for false positives involving:

- Products with similar names
- Your company versus your product
- Open-source packages with the same name
- Old product names or acquired products
- Your product appearing only in unrelated text

Require exact-match rules plus human review for ambiguous entities. Also confirm that URLs, descriptions and categories correspond to your actual SaaS.

## 9. Test whether your site can be discovered

Check:

- Relevant pages return `200`
- Important content is available without login
- Titles and descriptions clearly identify the product and use case
- Pricing and feature claims are current
- Canonical tags are correct
- Your robots/CDN configuration isn’t blocking relevant crawlers

For ChatGPT search, OpenAI says eligibility for summaries and citations depends in part on allowing `OAI-SearchBot`; placement is still not guaranteed. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search))

Also distinguish between **model-memory visibility** and **live-search visibility**. Being known without search is different from being found and cited during a current web search.

## 10. Recalculate the score yourself

From the raw export, independently calculate simple metrics:

- `% of prompts mentioning you`
- `% recommending you`
- `% citing you`
- `% containing material factual errors`
- Average rank when mentioned
- Share of mentions versus named competitors
- Stability across repeated runs

If your calculation cannot reproduce the headline number, ask the vendor to explain every transformation and weight.

## 11. Tie visibility to real outcomes

Compare trends against:

- ChatGPT referral sessions
- Grok/X referral sessions
- Sign-ups containing AI-referral attribution
- “How did you hear about us?” responses
- Branded-search lift
- Demo or trial quality

OpenAI adds `utm_source=chatgpt.com` to ChatGPT referral URLs, which can help identify inbound traffic, although visibility without a click will still be missed. ([help.openai.com](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq%23%3A~%3Atext%3DChatGPT%20automatically%20includes%20the%20UTM%20parameter%20utm_source%3Dchatgpt.com%20in%20referral%20URLs%2C%20enabling%20clear%20tracking%20and%20analysis%20of%20inbound%20traffic%20from%20ChatGPT%20search%20results))

## Fast trust checklist

I would trust a report only if it provides:

- [ ] Transparent formula  
- [ ] Exportable prompt set  
- [ ] Raw answers and citations  
- [ ] Exact model/search configuration  
- [ ] Multiple runs per prompt  
- [ ] Dates and version history  
- [ ] Failures included in the denominator  
- [ ] Branded and non-branded results separated  
- [ ] Mention, recommendation, accuracy and citation reported separately  
- [ ] A manually audited sample that you can reproduce  

**Bottom line:** optimize around a small, commercially meaningful prompt set and inspect the underlying answers. Use the aggregate score as a directional monitoring signal—not as proof of market share or customer exposure.
