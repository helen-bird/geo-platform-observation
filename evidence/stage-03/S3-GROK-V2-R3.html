Original prompt

As a bootstrapped SaaS builder, what checks should I perform before trusting reported ChatGPT and Grok visibility scores for my product?

The agreed prompt is reproduced exactly. Any recorded difference in the actual submitted text or search UI is retained in the raw record and full appendix.

Original answer

Do not treat a reported ChatGPT or Grok “visibility score” as a reliable, comparable number until you can verify the methodology, replicate key parts of it yourself, and separate the engines. There is no industry-standard score. Different tools use different formulas, prompt sets, engines, and weightings, and the underlying answers are noisy.

A recent benchmark found that the same questions produced top-five shortlist overlap of only about 17% across different AI engines (versus ~43% when the same engine was asked again), and one brand could score high on Claude/Grok while being nearly invisible on ChatGPT.⁠Markets.businessinsider

1. Demand (or reconstruct) the methodology

Ask for or find:

Exact prompt list (buyer-intent, unbranded queries are far more meaningful than “what is [your brand]?”)
Engines, models, and whether web search was on
Formula: simple mention rate (answers naming you ÷ total answers), position-weighted, sentiment-adjusted, or something else?
Sample size (number of answers, runs per prompt, date range)
How competitors were chosen

A score is only comparable to itself under the same conditions. Changing the prompt set, engine mix, or time window changes the number even if nothing about your product changed.⁠Brantial

2. Replicate it yourself (the highest-ROI check for a bootstrapped builder)

Build 10–20 high-intent prompts your actual customers would ask:

“Best [your category] tools for [specific use case]”
“[Problem] software for [team size / industry]”
“Alternatives to [known competitor]”
Comparison or “how to choose” questions

Then:

Run them in fresh ChatGPT and Grok sessions (new chats, memory cleared if possible, web search enabled).
Do this across several days.
Record for each answer: Was your product named? In what position / prominence? Was your site cited? Sentiment/accuracy? Which competitors appeared?

This costs almost nothing and often reveals that the reported score does not match what a real user sees. Multiple runs matter because the same prompt can produce different shortlists (“weather of the day” effects of ~14 points are common).⁠Getmint

3. Separate ChatGPT and Grok results

Blended scores hide important differences. ChatGPT and Grok draw from different sources and recommendation styles; Grok is more influenced by recent X/Twitter activity and engagement. A product can look strong on one and weak on the other. Always look at engine-level numbers, not just a composite.⁠Presenc

4. Check prompt relevance and bias
Do the prompts reflect real buyer language and intent in your category?
Do they favor well-known brands by design (broad category questions often do)?
Are branded prompts mixed in (these inflate scores toward 100%)?

Prefer unbranded, problem- or comparison-oriented prompts for meaningful measurement.

5. Evaluate reliability and noise
Small samples swing wildly. Prefer hundreds of answers over time rather than a one-off snapshot.
Day-to-day and run-to-run variance is normal. Look for trends over weeks, not single scores.
Note whether the tool accounts for stochasticity with repeated queries.
6. Look past the headline number

Useful supporting signals (often more actionable than the score itself):

Citation rate — does it link to your site or pages?
Position / prominence — first recommendation vs. a passing mention
Share of voice versus specific competitors
Sentiment and accuracy of the description
Actual AI referral traffic in your analytics (GA4 or similar)

Mentions without citations or positive recommendations have limited value.

7. Consider the source
Is this from an independent benchmark with public methodology and data, or a tool vendor marketing its own product?
Can you access raw answers or at least screenshots of key prompts?
How recent is the data? AI answers and models change.
Practical notes for bootstrapped SaaS

Manual spot-checks on a focused prompt set are often enough to decide whether a reported score is directionally useful or mostly noise. Many early-stage products score low or zero on broad category prompts; that is common and not always a crisis if your niche prompts or direct traffic perform well.

Focus next on the inputs that actually influence answers: clear entity presence, authoritative third-party mentions (reviews, docs, comparison content, communities), and (especially for Grok) relevant X activity. Track whether changes move the needle in your own repeated manual or low-cost automated checks rather than chasing a vendor’s composite score.

Bottom line: Treat any reported visibility score as a hypothesis. Verify the prompts and calculation, re-run the important queries yourself on both ChatGPT and Grok over a few days, and judge the result by whether the answers would actually help a buyer discover and choose your product.
