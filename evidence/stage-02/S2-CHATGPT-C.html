Original answer

Independent GEO experiments: ChatGPT Search vs Grok

Research cutoff: October 31, 2026

I found several published first-party studies that measure AI brand visibility across ChatGPT and Grok, including experiments by smaller GEO research teams and independent operators.

The key distinction is between recommendation experiments (which brands an AI suggests), citation experiments (which websites an AI references), and GEO intervention experiments (whether changing content causes AI visibility to improve).

These test different hypotheses. In particular, a study comparing ChatGPT and Grok does not automatically establish that it tested the actual consumer-facing ChatGPT Search interface.

1. Strongest published cross-platform experiments

1. mapou — State of AI Search 2026⁠￼

Original observational benchmark · May 2026

Compares which brands ChatGPT, Grok and three other engines recommend or cite across buyer-intent prompts.

Sample: 500 brands, 25 competitive segments, 5 engines, 20 canonical prompts per segment and 12 buyer personas. The authors report approximately 30,000 baseline brand-level verdicts per monthly run.

Method: Fixed prompts spanning discovery, filtered discovery, comparison and evaluation. Measures brand visibility, engine agreement and the effects of buyer personas, with Wilson 95% confidence intervals.

Finding: Mean cross-engine brand-ranking agreement was reported at 52%. AI visibility changes materially by engine and buyer persona.

Evidence status: Published comparative measurement with a methodology. ChatGPT Search interface equivalence is not established by the headline report. mapou

2. AI Visibility Report Group — Six AI Platforms, Same Eight Questions⁠￼

Original repeated citation test · August 29, 2026

Direct comparison of ChatGPT, Grok, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity and Meta AI.

Sample: Eight unbranded buying questions, six platforms, two rounds. Of 96 planned responses, 87 were successful (39 in round one, 48 in round two).

Method: Ask identical buyer questions separately, extract citation URLs, normalize sources to domains and compare overlap.

Finding: In the complete second round, 468 of 551 cited domains (85%) appeared on just one platform. Grok cited 256 domains and ChatGPT 50.

Evidence status: Verified published experiment on citation-source diversity. Small sample, restricted to Dutch-market AI visibility questions. The figures do not measure the number of brands recommended. research

3. GOSH AI — The Search vs Learned Divergence⁠￼

Original multi-model observational study · March–May 2026

Tests whether brands surfaced through live retrieval differ from brands generated using learned model knowledge.

Sample: Six AI-model families, including ChatGPT and Grok; more than 23,800 model executions, 80,300 citations and 8,600 brands identified across the reported baseline.

Method: Apply equivalent prompts with retrieval enabled and disabled, then classify observed brand-category pairs by presence in each path.

Finding: Only 8.28% of observed brand-category pairs appeared in both the live-search and learned-knowledge paths.

Evidence status: Published original research with substantial reported sample size. The headline result is aggregated across models, and the comparable dual-path category analysis is narrower. It should not be presented as a ChatGPT-vs-Grok-specific effect. GOSH AI

2. Individual builders running original tests

These are particularly relevant if your goal is to identify people publishing their own experiments rather than relying on generic GEO advice.

4. Zeeshan Yaseen — Two GEO experiments challenging conventional AI visibility advice

First-person field experiments · Published September 14, 2026

Read the original experiment write-up⁠￼

Method and sample: Two sequential tests, each using 15 commercial-intent keywords. The first compared four engines and the second added Grok and Google AI Mode, for six engines total. The second was a 30-day cold-start campaign for a SaaS link-building agency, with manual checks and tracked content placements.

Result: In the second experiment, the tracked brand appeared 32 times on ChatGPT versus 4 times on Grok, out of 298 appearances across all six platforms. Both experiments together logged 775 citation events.

Assessment: A genuine first-person GEO intervention study involving both engines. It has commercial interventions and before/after measurements, but no randomized control or established ChatGPT Search interface matching. Search Engine Land

5. Charles Cousyn / Weftd — ChatGPT, Instinct, Grok Bot: Our Test

Independent real-world recommendation test · September 23, 2026

Read the restaurant recommendation experiment⁠￼

Method and sample: The builder gave the same practical mission to ChatGPT, Grok Bot and Instinct: identify three restaurants near Lyon Part-Dieu station for a business lunch with a specified reservation requirement. Five agent configurations were tested on September 22–23.

Result: The configurations suggested 12 different restaurants, with only one appearing in more than two configurations. The one reservation verified in depth exposed contradictory availability information.

Assessment: A first-person, real-world comparison of AI recommendations, especially useful for local GEO. The sample is just five configurations for one mission. The research tests agent behavior, not a broad SEO optimization intervention. Weftd

6. Queraid — AI Visibility Study 2026

Original brand recommendation benchmark · May 25, 2026

Read the 70+ brand experiment⁠￼

Method and sample: Over 70 brands across more than 50 industries, evaluated using six prompts per brand on each of five engines, including ChatGPT and Grok. The authors report more than 2,000 responses.

The prompts cover brand identity, recommendations, comparisons, reputation, use cases and best-in-class selections. Scores combine mention rate (40%), sentiment (30%), competitor parity (20%) and cross-model consistency (10%).

Assessment: Published first-party quantitative benchmarking. The composite score is proprietary to the researchers, and the reported headline rankings do not separately isolate ChatGPT Search versus Grok. The authors say the underlying dataset is available through their API. Queraid

4. Two additional studies worth including

7. Nazareno Castro Bay / Rank Prompt — Same brand, different AI surfaces

Original test and methodology⁠￼

This is one of the closest matches to your request because the authors explicitly separate ChatGPT Search, ChatGPT without search, and Grok.

On July 30, 2026, they tested one B2B brand using 70 prompts across seven engine surfaces, producing 490 prompt-by-engine observations. They held the country, language, date and brand constant.

They reported:

* 5.71% brand presence on Grok versus 0% on ChatGPT overall.
* 25.5 citations per answer on Grok versus 4.2 on ChatGPT Search.
* 87.45% of cited URLs appeared on exactly one engine for the same question.

Evidence status: Original, documented experiment with explicit search-mode separation. However, the test concerns one brand and one market category, and the published collection uses the company’s API rather than a verified consumer-interface session. Rank Prompt

8. Columbia University Tow Center — ChatGPT Search vs Grok source accuracy

Read the Tow Center’s original study⁠￼

This is an independent academic-journalism benchmark, published March 6, 2025.

Sample: 20 news publishers × 10 articles × 8 AI search tools = 1,600 queries, with 200 queries per tool.

Method: Researchers supplied article excerpts and asked each AI search engine to identify the article, publisher and URL. They manually assessed accuracy.

Results: ChatGPT Search incorrectly identified 134 of 200 articles (67%). Grok-3 answered 94% incorrectly.

Evidence status: Strong direct evidence about retrieval and citation accuracy, with explicitly named ChatGPT Search and Grok versions. It does not test commercial GEO brand recommendations. Columbia Journalism Review

Conclusion

For your specific research question, I would prioritize Rank Prompt, mapou, Zeeshan Yaseen, and Charles Cousyn.

Rank Prompt provides the clearest reported distinction between ChatGPT Search and Grok. mapou has the broader brand recommendation panel. Yaseen documents an actual GEO campaign intervention. Cousyn provides a concrete, first-person comparison of real-world local business recommendations.

The evidence supports measurable differences across AI systems in brand visibility and citation selection. It does not yet establish a universal GEO tactic that reliably increases recommendations on both ChatGPT Search and Grok. A stronger demonstration would require identical pre-registered buyer prompts, repeated runs, documented interface versions, publicly available raw responses and a controlled content intervention.
