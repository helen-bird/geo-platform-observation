Original answer

Independent builders have published a handful of original, data-backed experiments comparing GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) tactics and outcomes across ChatGPT Search and Grok. Most public discussion remains opinion or anecdotal. The verified experiments below are distinguished by disclosed methods, sample sizes, and direct links to primary write-ups or data. Larger academic or vendor studies (e.g., Princeton GEO-bench, Fudan fabricated-concept paper, Peec AI fanout analysis) are noted only where they contextualize, as they are not from independent builders.

Kasra Dash (@Kasra_Dash) — Self-referencing listicles, comparisons, and alternatives

Direct link: X post with setup, results, and data images (April 2026; also covered in interviews such as Ryan Doser’s video).

Methodology: Published 50 self-referencing articles on a mid-age domain in three batches (roughly 10, then 20, then 20), with ~3 weeks between drops. Formats: comparison pages (e.g., brand A vs brand B), listicles (“best X for Y”), and alternative pages (“Z alternatives”). Tracked brand mentions/citations across ChatGPT, Grok, Perplexity, Claude, and Google AI Overviews. Later 410’d (removed) all pages and monitored retention of citations.

Sample size: 50 articles; multi-week observation with batch-level tracking of mentions (e.g., Grok mentions scaled from ~7 → 25 → 41 across batches).

Key verified findings (vs. ChatGPT Search): Grok and Perplexity picked up the brand fastest and most readily (high single-digit to low double-digit “out of 10” style pickup rates in summaries; Grok highly responsive). ChatGPT and Claude were slower/more moderate (~6/10 range in secondary reports) but retained references to deleted pages for weeks, while Grok and Perplexity dropped faster after 410s. Google AI was least responsive. This is presented as a live, tracked test rather than theory; data visuals are in the primary post.

This is a clear original experiment by an independent SEO/AI-search practitioner.

Lennart Vallo / Cited (wearecited.com) — New-domain niche content to #1 citations

Direct link: Full results write-up (April 2026 case study on BaliPropertyRules.com).

Methodology: Registered a blank domain (early February 2026), published 18 original articles on Bali property regulations/costs/processes with technical/strategic optimization and no link building, paid placements, or ads. Tracked citations via 10 audits over ~6–8 weeks (Feb 12–Mar 29, 2026) using a fixed set of prompts. Measured coverage (% of prompts citing the site), total citations, share of voice, win rate (first-position citations), domain rank, unique URLs, and referral traffic (GA4 + GSC). Also noted volatility and response to a Google Core Update.

Sample size: 18 articles; 73 prompts across the engines; 10 audits; 202 total citations recorded.

Key verified findings (ChatGPT Search vs. Grok):

Grok: Higher coverage (~72.6%), more citations (68), broader unique URLs (~20), strong/high win rate (cited first in a large majority of cases when it cited the site), #1 domain rank. More volatile but loyal and broad in trust from early on.
ChatGPT/OpenAI: Lower coverage (~28.8%), fewer citations (25), fewer unique URLs (~11), still #1 domain rank on the prompts it covered, but narrower (favored information-scarcity/time-sensitive angles) and more correlated with Google signals. Drove more tracked referral sessions in this test despite lower coverage.

Site reached #1 domain rank on all four engines tested (including ChatGPT and Grok). Data is summarized in tables in the public write-up (no raw CSV download noted, but methodology and metrics are disclosed). This is an original controlled-style experiment by an independent/small-team builder.

GEO takeaways include strong “niche-boxing” for Grok (high specialty association, low general-mention rate ~13.5%, niche-boxing ~78.7%) versus ChatGPT’s default/generalist strength. More observational ranking experiment than content-tactic test, but original and quantified.

GEO Log and related pages. Stronger on controlled single-variable tests and methodology transparency than pure ChatGPT-vs-Grok head-to-heads, but independent and data-driven.
Verified experiments vs. general opinions

Verified experiments (above) publish concrete methods, sample sizes, tracking periods, and (at least summarized) results or data visuals. They allow inspection of what was actually tested (listicle formats, new-domain niche content, structural changes, etc.) and how ChatGPT Search and Grok differed in speed of pickup, breadth of citation, retention after content removal, volatility, or ranking style.

General opinions dominate elsewhere: blog posts, X threads, and agency advice asserting that “listicles work,” “add statistics/quotes,” “Grok is easier,” or “ChatGPT prefers X” without disclosed sample sizes, controls, tracking windows, or primary data. Many recycle the original Princeton GEO paper’s simulated findings (top-5 Google results fed to an older model) without live multi-engine tests. Company tools (Qwairy, VectorGap, AthenaHQ, Promptwatch, etc.) produce large observational datasets that include both engines but are product/vendor reports rather than independent builder experiments.

Caveats that apply across the independent work: Sample sizes are modest by academic standards (tens of articles or prompts, not tens of thousands); engines update frequently, so results are time-bound; most are not peer-reviewed; and “success” is usually measured in citations/mentions rather than downstream traffic or conversions (though some track the latter). Retrieval behavior and model knowledge often matter more than on-page tweaks alone, per larger studies.

These are the clearest publicly documented independent-builder experiments that directly compare GEO outcomes on ChatGPT Search versus Grok. Primary sources remain the Kasra Dash X thread and the Cited results page.
