Original answer

Here are the strongest free resources for learning Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) in 2026, based on practitioner roundups, course comparisons, and active sources. GEO focuses on getting content cited, quoted, or recommended in AI-generated answers (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, Gemini, Claude, etc.).⁠Stackmatix⁠Rankprompt

1. Foundational Research (Start Here)
Original GEO paper: GEO: Generative Engine Optimization by Aggarwal et al. (Princeton, IIT Delhi, and others), arXiv:2311.09735, published at KDD 2024.
This is the primary source that coined the term. It tested optimization strategies (e.g., adding statistics, quotations, citations) across thousands of queries and showed meaningful visibility gains (roughly 30–40% on key metrics for evidence-based edits; keyword stuffing performed poorly). Free on arXiv. Follow-up comparative studies (e.g., arXiv:2509.08919) examine differences across engines.⁠Thegeocommunity⁠arXiv
2. Best Free Structured Courses

These are short, practical, and updated for 2026:

3. Curated Learning Paths & Communities
The GEO Community (thegeocommunity.com): One of the strongest free hubs in 2026. Features a structured /start learning path (role-based tracks for Strategist, Builder, Engineer), regularly updated practitioner blog (crawlability, schema, LLMs.txt, entity strategy, experiments), and documented tests. Often recommended as a better-organized alternative to many paid courses.⁠Thegeocommunity
LearningAIsearch.com by Aleyda Solis: Free, curated roadmap of reliable third-party guides covering fundamentals, measurement, technical, content, prompt research, ecommerce, and more. Continuously updated; no ads or paid placements. Complements her broader LearningSEO.io resource.⁠Frankchiu
Class Central: “25 Resources to Learn Generative Engine Optimization in 2026” roundup (courses, blogs, YouTube, guides). Good overview of the landscape.
4. Free Ebooks, Guides & Official Docs
The GEO Lab (thegeolab.net/ebooks/): Multiple free ebooks (Gumroad $0 or direct reads), including the 2026 Pocket Guide, SEO-to-GEO Complete Framework, Experiments guide, Workbook, WordPress technical guide, Authority Playbook, and more. Built around their GEO Stack framework.
Google Search Central documentation on AI Overviews, AI Mode, and structured data — authoritative baseline for Google’s ecosystem.
Practitioner blogs and guides (e.g., Neil Patel GEO content, Stackmatix free resources roundup, various agency deep-dives).
5. Ongoing Learning
YouTube channels with practical demos (Nathan Gotch, Exposure Ninja, SMA Marketing, Writesonic, etc.).
Communities: Relevant Reddit/Discord/Slack groups, LinkedIn discussions of real experiments, and practitioner newsletters.
Measurement basics: Google Search Console (now with generative AI performance reports in 2026) + GA4 + manual prompt tracking in spreadsheets.
Recommended Learning Path (2026)
Read the original arXiv paper (or a solid practitioner summary of it).
Complete the Ahrefs AEO course (lowest friction) + one practical course (Apollo Digital or Rank Prompt).
Work through The GEO Community /start path or LearningAIsearch.com roadmap.
Download a couple of GEO Lab ebooks or checklists.
Apply it: Run an AI visibility audit on a site you control, optimize a few pages, track citations/mentions over 4–8 weeks, and iterate.

Key advice from 2026 sources: Prefer resources from active practitioners sharing real citation data over pure theory. Combine official docs, short courses, and communities. Hands-on measurement on your own site beats certificates. The field moves fast—check recency (ideally updated within the last 6 months) and multi-engine coverage.⁠Stackmatix

Most of the above are free with no or minimal signup. Start with the paper + Ahrefs course if you want the quickest solid foundation.
