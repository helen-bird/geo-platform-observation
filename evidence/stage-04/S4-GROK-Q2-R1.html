Original prompt

What credible experiments have measured whether GEO tactics increase AI brand mentions or recommendations? Include sources when available.

The agreed prompt is reproduced exactly. Any recorded difference in the actual submitted text or search UI is retained in the raw record and full appendix.

Original answer

The most credible controlled experiments are academic benchmarks (especially the foundational Princeton/KDD 2024 GEO study), with supporting work from later papers, large-scale observational analyses, and some industry case studies. Evidence is stronger for increasing the share of text or prominence of already-retrieved content inside AI answers than for boosting organic retrieval, brand discovery, or cross-platform causal effects. A 2026 critical survey of ~45 studies concluded that GEO tactics reliably change how an already-retrieved page is cited/used, but no reviewed technique shows a stable, longitudinal, cross-platform causal effect on organic discoverability.⁠Geoaura

1. Princeton GEO Study (Foundational Peer-Reviewed Work)

Paper: “GEO: Generative Engine Optimization” by Pranjal Aggarwal et al. (IIT Delhi, Princeton, etc.), presented at KDD 2024 (arXiv:2311.09735).⁠arXiv

Design: GEO-bench with ~10,000 real-world-style queries across domains + relevant web sources. They tested nine content rewrite tactics on sources already in a fixed retrieval context (top results fed to a generative engine setup, also validated on Perplexity). Visibility was measured primarily as position-adjusted word count (share of the answer text attributable to the source, weighted by position) and a subjective impression metric.
Key results (relative lifts on position-adjusted word count vs. baseline):
Quotation/expert quotation addition: ~+41% (highest; e.g., 19.3 → 27.2 in reported figures).
Statistics addition: ~+33%.
Fluency optimization: ~+29%.
Citing sources: ~+28%.
Other style/language tweaks: smaller positive effects.
Keyword stuffing: −8% (negative).
Gains were largest for lower-ranked sources (e.g., rank-5 sources saw ~100%+ lifts with citation/quotation/statistics methods; top-ranked sources sometimes lost share). Effects varied by domain. On Perplexity they saw up to ~37% gains on some metrics.
Caveat: This measures absorption/visibility inside the answer once the source is already retrieved and in context. It does not test improving initial retrieval or discoverability. Later replications and critiques note the numbers are conditional on the fixed testbed and metric.⁠Geoaura

This remains the most-cited rigorous experiment establishing that certain content edits (evidence-rich additions) can increase a source’s presence in generated answers.

2. Product Recommendation Manipulation Experiment

Paper: “Manipulating Large Language Models to Increase Product Visibility” by Aounon Kumar and Himabindu Lakkaraju (arXiv:2404.07981, 2024).⁠arXiv

Design: Catalog of fictitious coffee machines. They added carefully crafted “strategic text sequences” (STS) to product pages and measured changes in LLM top recommendations for two target products (one rarely appearing, one typically ranking second).
Results: STS significantly raised the chance of both targets appearing as the top recommendation. This demonstrated that content on a product page can manipulatively increase LLM recommendation rates in a controlled setting.
Relevance: Direct measurement of increased product recommendations via GEO-style content changes.
3. C-SEO Bench (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks)

Paper: “C-SEO Bench: Does Conversational SEO Work?” by Haritz Puerto et al. (arXiv:2506.11097).⁠arXiv

Design: Tested content rewrite methods (including several from the Princeton paper) plus traditional ranking approaches on question-answering and product-recommendation tasks across domains. Metric focused on citation rank in the output, with varying numbers of competing actors.
Results: Most content-level C-SEO/GEO rewrites were largely ineffective or negative on citation ranking. Traditional SEO approaches that improve a source’s ranking/position in the LLM’s context were substantially more effective. Gains diminished as more actors adopted the same tactics (zero-sum/congested).

This challenges some of the earlier positive findings when the metric shifts from word-share to ranking under competition.

4. Large-Scale Observational and Other Academic Work
Kumar et al. (arXiv:2606.20065, 2026): Analysis of 100,000+ prompt responses across 100+ brands. Established a clear brand-stature ladder (household names cited in ~73% of relevant answers, mid-market ~44%, niche ~11%). Ranked “best-of” listicles were a top citation format (~21%). Provides a baseline more than a controlled tactic experiment.⁠Geoaura
University of Toronto work (Chen, Koudas et al.): Multiple papers showing strong earned-media (third-party) bias over brand-owned content in AI search citations (often 6.5× advantage for third-party platforms). A 2026 preprint (“What Drives AI Citations?”) on 158k+ answers found that page-level tactics (FAQ blocks, schema, summary boxes, etc.) showed little or no within-website association with citations once retrieval is controlled; retrieval itself explained far more variance. Brand names also influence rankings even with identical facts.⁠Aivisibilityjournal
Seer Interactive GEO Olympics Study (2026): 231,347+ LLM responses across 7 platforms over 52 days of Olympics data. Tested hypotheses on signals (entity authority, third-party validation, community discussion). Strong signal stacks produced ~7.8× more AI mentions. Different sources drive listing vs. recommendation; platform-specific patterns (e.g., Reddit, editorial, official sites) were clear. Large-scale observational rather than pure controlled experiment.⁠Seerinteractive
Other: E-GEO e-commerce testbed; VoteGEO-Bench on electoral visibility; various GEO-Bench ranking-manipulation studies; Stacker/Scrunch earned-media distribution tests reporting large citation lifts from third-party placement.
Industry Experiments and Case Studies (Less Controlled, but Directional)
SIXT (via Peec AI, 2026): Controlled experiment editing partner listings (not their own site) on high-traffic travel domains. Full-treatment domains saw ~320% increase in citation frequency (adjusted ~308%); control rose only ~12%. Also gains in visibility and share of voice.⁠Peec
Search Engine Land experiments (2026): Multi-month and 30-day tests tracking commercial keywords across platforms. Reported upward trends in citations via PR, listicles, guest posts, and updates; one cold-start produced hundreds of appearances. One original conclusion did not fully hold on retest.⁠Searchengineland
OtterlyAI cold-start: New brand with zero authority reached competitive share of voice / rank ~7 via external mentions/directories in 14 days.
Ahrefs and others: Large correlation studies (e.g., 75k brands) linking unlinked brand mentions strongly to AI visibility (correlations ~0.65–0.71), stronger than raw backlinks.
Some schema/JSON-LD and certain page-feature tests have returned null or mixed results in controlled designs.

Overall takeaway from the evidence: Adding statistics, quotations, citations, and fluency improvements can increase a source’s share and prominence inside AI answers when the content is already retrieved (Princeton results). Being retrieved in the first place (via relevance, third-party mentions, listicles, earned media, and model prior knowledge) appears more decisive for brand mentions and recommendations. Effects are platform- and domain-dependent, often stronger for lower-ranked or lesser-known brands, and can be zero-sum under competition. Keyword stuffing is consistently unhelpful. Most commercial claims of large, general “GEO lifts” exceed what the controlled experiments rigorously support.
