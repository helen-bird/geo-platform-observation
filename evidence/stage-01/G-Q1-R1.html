Original answer

The best free resources for learning Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) in 2026 combine foundational research, official platform guidance, structured courses, curated learning paths, and practitioner content. GEO focuses on making content more likely to be retrieved, cited, or mentioned in AI-generated answers (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, Gemini, etc.), overlapping heavily with Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) and traditional SEO fundamentals.⁠Stackmatix

Foundational Research (Start Here)
Original GEO paper: GEO: Generative Engine Optimization by Aggarwal et al. (arXiv:2311.09735, published at KDD 2024). This is the primary source that named the field. It defines visibility metrics, introduces GEO-bench, and shows that certain content strategies can increase citation/visibility rates by ~30–40%. Free to read on arXiv.⁠arXiv
Follow-up research (e.g., comparative studies across engines like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini) is also available on arXiv for deeper technical context.
Official Documentation
Google Search Central: “Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search” (and related pages on AI features/AI Overviews). Google’s position is that standard SEO best practices (helpful, reliable, people-first content; technical eligibility for indexing and snippets) still apply. There are no special requirements or “GEO hacks” (such as llms.txt) needed for AI Overviews or AI Mode. This is the authoritative baseline for Google’s ecosystem.⁠Developers.google
Bing Webmaster Tools also provides free AI citation and visibility insights.
Free Structured Courses

These are practical, self-paced, and require no payment (some may need a free account for certificates):

Apollo Digital Free GEO Course — ~7 lessons / 100 minutes across foundations, technical readiness (crawlers, robots.txt), measurement (with free Google Sheets templates for audits and share-of-voice tracking), and tooling. No signup required; progress saves in-browser. Updated 2026.⁠Apollodigital
Ahrefs Academy AEO Course — 12 lessons / ~1 hour 26 minutes. Covers how AI search works, platform differences (ChatGPT, AI Overviews, Perplexity), content that gets cited, brand mentions, technical practices, and measurement. No signup required to watch; free Ahrefs certification exam available.⁠Ahrefs
HubSpot Academy AEO Fundamentals — Free certification course (~2 hours 20 minutes). Focuses on answer engines, metrics (visibility, share of voice, citations), content planning, and measurement. There’s also a related “SEO in the Age of AI” path.⁠Offers.hubspot
Other solid free options: Open University’s short (~1-hour) intro to GEO; Rank Prompt’s free ~90-minute GEO & AEO course; AISQ Growth’s GEO Academy; and Finseo Academy materials (video lessons, some with certificates).
Curated Learning Paths, Guides & Ebooks
LearningAIsearch.com (by Aleyda Solis) — Free, non-biased roadmap that organizes reliable free guides and resources across fundamentals, measurement, technical, content, and tools. Maintained by a respected international SEO/AI search consultant; also ties into the SEOFOMO newsletter.⁠Learningaisearch
Class Central: “25 Resources to Learn Generative Engine Optimization in 2026” — Aggregates free/paid courses, guides (e.g., iPullRank AI Search Manual), blogs (Neil Patel, Seer Interactive, Writesonic, Profound), YouTube channels (Nathan Gotch, Exposure Ninja, Ahrefs), and podcasts. Updated into 2026.⁠Classcentral
Stackmatix: Free AEO and GEO Learning Resources in 2026 — Practical overview of resource types (official docs, practitioner blogs, communities, research) plus a suggested multi-week learning path (fundamentals → competitive landscape → tactics → apply & measure).⁠Stackmatix
The GEO Lab free ebooks — Multiple free downloads (Pocket Guide 2026 Edition, SEO-to-GEO framework, experiments guide, 30-day workbook, technical WordPress guide, glossary, authority playbook, and full AI SEO OS). Based on their GEO Stack framework.⁠Thegeolab
Practitioner Blogs, Video, Communities & Tools
Blogs & newsletters: Neil Patel’s GEO content, Seer Interactive, Writesonic GEO archives, Profound, daydream, TripleDart, and Aleyda Solis’s SEOFOMO.
YouTube: Nathan Gotch, Ahrefs channel (AEO playlist), Exposure Ninja, SMA Marketing, and Finseo Academy playlists for short practical lessons.
Communities: Relevant Reddit communities, Discord/Slack groups focused on AI search (look for places sharing real citation data rather than pure theory).
Free measurement/tools: Google Search Console (AI impressions within Web search data), Bing Webmaster Tools (AI citations), free tiers or audits from various visibility checkers, plus spreadsheets for manual prompt tracking. Practice by running a fixed set of buyer prompts across engines before/after changes on a site you control.
Recommended Learning Path (2026)
Read the original GEO paper + Google’s official AI features docs for a clear baseline.
Complete one structured free course (Ahrefs AEO or Apollo Digital are strong starting points).
Use LearningAIsearch.com or the Class Central/Stackmatix roundups as hubs.
Apply tactics on a few pages, measure with free tools (prompt tracking + Search Console), and iterate.
Stay current via practitioner newsletters and communities—GEO evolves quickly as engines change.

Caveats: No single official “GEO certification” exists; certificates prove study more than results. The strongest signal is measurable before/after citation or mention improvements on prompts you control. Google’s stance is that core SEO still dominates for its AI features, while multi-engine strategies (content extractability, entity clarity, earned mentions, technical crawlability) matter more broadly. Quality varies—prioritize sources with real data or experiments over pure theory.⁠Pixis

These resources are free as of late 2026 and should give a solid, practical foundation without paid courses or tools.
