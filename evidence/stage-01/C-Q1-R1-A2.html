Original answer

Best free resources to learn GEO in 2026

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of improving the likelihood that AI-powered search and answer systems — such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews — discover, cite, or recommend your content.

My recommendation is to learn GEO through three complementary approaches: official platform documentation, original research, and hands-on experiments with measurable outcomes. GEO is evolving quickly, and many commercial guides promote tactics whose effectiveness has not been independently established.

For a free learning path, I would prioritize the following resources.

1. Best resources at a glance

1. Google Search Central — Best official guidance
Start here
Free · Official documentation · Beginner to intermediate
Explains how Google's AI search features discover and surface content, why conventional SEO still matters, and which popular GEO claims Google does not endorse.

https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide

These resources serve different purposes. Google is the strongest source for Google's own requirements; academic papers provide research methods and evidence; courses offer a more convenient route to implementation. No single resource establishes a universal formula for getting recommended by every AI system.

Official visibility measurement
• Google Search Console / AI features: measures impressions from supported Google AI search features.
• Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance: reports citations, cited pages, and associated grounding queries across supported AI experiences.
Both are free. They measure different surfaces and require a website you can access and verify.

3. What does the research actually support?

The original GEO paper by Aggarwal and colleagues, published at KDD 2024, is especially important because it evaluates optimization tactics experimentally. It reports visibility improvements of up to 40% on its benchmark, with results varying by domain. That is a benchmark result, not a promise of a 40% improvement for every website or AI platform.

Google's 2026 guidance adds an important practical constraint: conventional SEO remains foundational, and Google explicitly cautions against relying on supposed GEO hacks such as unnecessary llms.txt files, artificial mentions, or rigid content-chunking rules.

Use these principles to evaluate any tutorial:
• Evidence: Does it report an actual experiment, sample size, baseline, and outcome?
• Attribution: Can the author distinguish a content change from model updates or changes in search demand?
• Reproducibility: Are the prompts, measurement method, and evaluation period documented?
• Commercial incentives: Is the author selling a tool whose effectiveness the tutorial claims to demonstrate?
• Platform specificity: Does the advice work on multiple systems, or only on one particular search experience?

4. Recommended learning plan: two weeks

For an independent builder, I would spend less time collecting courses and more time publishing and measuring original work.

Days 1–2
Learn the fundamentals
Read Google's guide and complete the Ahrefs AEO course. Write a one-page summary of how AI discovery differs from traditional search.

Days 3–4
Read the original paper
Identify the tested tactics, outcome measures, experimental limitations, and assumptions that might not hold in current AI systems.

Days 5–7
Run a baseline experiment
Select 10–20 questions relevant to your topic. Test them across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI search where available. Record brand mentions, citations, cited URLs, and competitors.

Days 8–10
Publish one controlled content change
For example, improve a post with original observations, verifiable evidence, and clear sourcing. Keep the topic and other conditions as consistent as possible.

Days 11–14
Re-test and publish your findings
Repeat the same prompts, document changes and variability, and publish a short experiment report. A null result is useful if the method is transparent.

One methodological caution: AI answers can vary between runs. Use repeated tests, a consistent prompt set, and a record of the test date and platform. A simple before-and-after comparison is a useful starting point, but it does not establish causality on its own.

My final shortlist

If you only have time for three resources, choose these:
1. Google Search Central for official platform guidance.
2. The original GEO paper for research-backed methods and limitations.
3. Ahrefs' free AEO course for a structured, practical introduction.

Then start testing those methods on content you actually publish. That will teach you more about what works in your own context than completing several overlapping courses.
