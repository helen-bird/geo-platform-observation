# What credible experiments show about GEO effectiveness

As of October 2026, the evidence suggests that **GEO tactics can improve visibility in AI-generated answers, but the strength of evidence varies substantially by tactic and study design.** The clearest experimental evidence comes from academic research on content optimization. Large-scale industry studies support the importance of third-party brand mentions, while commercial case studies report substantial improvements after coordinated GEO campaigns.

The central distinction is whether a study demonstrates that a tactic *causes* more AI recommendations or merely shows that brands using that tactic tend to receive more mentions.

## 1\. The strongest evidence, ranked by study design

**1\. Princeton researchers — GEO: Generative Engine Optimization (KDD 2024)**

Peer-reviewed academic paper · Published August 2024

The researchers introduced GEO-bench and systematically tested content-optimization strategies across diverse queries and domains. They reported that optimization could improve content visibility in generative-engine responses by **up to 40%**\. Tested approaches included adding relevant citations, statistics and quotations, and improving the presentation of information.

**What it establishes\:** Content-level changes can influence how generative engines represent and surface information under experimental conditions.

**Limitations\:** The headline is a maximum result, not an average guaranteed lift. The outcome is content visibility, which does not necessarily mean more brand recommendations, purchases or conversions.

Source: urlKDD 2024 paper and DOIhttps://doi.org/10.1145/3637528.3671900 · urlPrinceton research recordhttps://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/geo-generative-engine-optimization/\.

**3\. Ahrefs — Brand visibility factors across 75,000 brands**

Industry data analysis · 2025

Ahrefs analyzed millions of AI-generated responses and found that brand mentions across the web correlated strongly with mentions in Google's AI Overviews. Its May 2025 study reported a Spearman correlation of **0.664** for web mentions, compared with **0.218** for backlinks and **0.392** for branded search volume.

A later study extended the analysis to ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews, again finding strong associations between third-party brand mentions and AI visibility.

**What it establishes\:** Off-site brand presence is a promising GEO signal worth testing.

**Limitations\:** Correlation cannot establish whether additional mentions cause AI visibility to rise. Larger, more established brands may naturally have both more mentions and greater AI visibility.

Sources: url2025 studyhttps://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/ · urlCross-platform follow-up studyhttps://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/\.

## 2\. What does the evidence say about individual GEO tactics?

The studies above support different conclusions. The table below separates tactics with experimental support from those with mainly observational or commercial evidence.

| GEO tactic | Evidence | Current conclusion |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Add statistics, citations and expert quotations | Princeton controlled evaluation | Promising content-level intervention; test in your own niche |
| Earn third-party brand mentions | Large-scale Ahrefs correlation analysis | Strong association; causal effect remains uncertain |
| Get included in “best of” lists and comparison articles | Commercial case studies and AI citation analyses | Promising for product recommendations; limited controlled evidence |
| Publish more pages | Ahrefs observational analysis | Weak evidence that page volume alone increases AI visibility |
| Build backlinks | Ahrefs observational analysis | Weaker association than brand mentions in its 2025 AI Overview study |
| Publish social content | Comparative AI search research | Effects vary by platform; social content may be less influential than earned media |

Two additional sources help explain these findings.

**4\. Chen et al. — *Generative Engine Optimization: How to Dominate AI Search* \(2025)**

This arXiv preprint uses large-scale controlled experiments to compare AI search with traditional search across verticals, languages and query variations. It finds that AI search systems favor earned media—third-party authoritative sources—over brand-owned and social content, with substantial differences between engines. urlRead the paperhttps://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919\.

The distinction matters: this research provides evidence about *which sources AI systems tend to use*, but it does not establish that purchasing or securing a particular media placement will cause a brand to receive more recommendations.

## 3\. How to interpret the evidence correctly

Three measurement distinctions are essential when evaluating GEO claims.

**Brand mention rate**

How frequently an AI answer names the brand. This is the most direct measure of whether a brand appears at all.

**Recommendation and position**

Whether the AI actually recommends the brand, places it among the top options, or merely mentions it in passing. A citation alone does not establish a positive recommendation.

**Business impact**

Whether the intervention increases qualified visits, leads or purchases. Greater AI visibility does not automatically translate into commercial results.

AI answers can also change across repeated runs of the same prompt. Semrush explicitly describes this non-determinism in its measurement discussion. Consequently, before-and-after comparisons based on only a handful of prompts can produce misleading conclusions. urlSource and methodology discussionhttps://www.semrush.com/blog/how-we-are-using-semrush-to-drive-llm-visibility/\.

## 4\. What would constitute convincing evidence?

For someone conducting independent GEO experiments, I would prioritize the following design.

1. **Establish a baseline.** Select 30–100 realistic prompts in one topic area and record brand mentions, recommendation rate, ranking within recommendations and citations.
2. **Change one variable at a time.** For example, test a structured, evidence-rich article against a comparable article without the optimization. Alternatively, compare prompts associated with earned-media placements against a matched control group.
3. **Repeat across multiple runs.** Use the same prompts, models, settings and observation schedule. Record changes in the AI system and its retrieval sources.
4. **Measure the treatment effect.** Compare changes in the treated group with changes in the control group, ideally using a difference-in-differences design. For content experiments, account for differences in topic, baseline authority and publication timing.
5. **Publish the results, including failures.** Share the prompt set, intervention, sample size, observation period, outcome definitions and uncertainty. This makes the findings independently testable.

## Bottom line

The strongest conclusion currently supported by the evidence is that **GEO is measurable and content optimization can improve AI visibility, but reliable causal evidence for many popular tactics remains limited**\.

For practical experimentation, I would start with two hypotheses\:

1. **Content-level optimization\:** Does adding concrete statistics, attributable citations and useful comparisons increase the probability that an AI answer surfaces your content or mentions your brand?
2. **Earned-media optimization\:** Does getting your brand included in relevant third-party comparisons increase its recommendation rate relative to a matched control?

These hypotheses connect the academic evidence to real-world brand outcomes, and both can be tested without relying on vendor claims or assuming that correlation proves causation.
