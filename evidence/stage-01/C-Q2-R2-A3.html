Original answer

What credible experiments show about GEO and AI brand visibility

As of October 30, 2026, the evidence supports a qualified conclusion: GEO tactics can influence what AI systems cite and recommend under controlled conditions, but reliable, lasting increases in a real brand’s organic visibility across AI search engines remain difficult to establish.

The key is to distinguish three outcomes:

Brand mentions: Does ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Perplexity name a brand?

Citations: Does the answer link to a brand’s website or a third-party page mentioning it?

Recommendations: Does the AI actually recommend the brand or product to the user?

These metrics are related, but an improvement in one does not establish an improvement in the others.

1. The strongest evidence, ranked by experimental rigor
1. Princeton University — GEO: Generative Engine Optimization (2024)
Peer-reviewed research

Finding: GEO methods improved visibility by up to 40% in the researchers’ experimental setup. 
Princeton University
+1

The researchers introduced GEO-bench, a benchmark of queries and relevant web sources, and compared optimized content with unoptimized content. Methods included adding relevant statistics, quotations, authoritative citations, and other content modifications.

Important limitation: The experiment operated within a controlled retrieval-and-generation pipeline. It demonstrates that changing content can improve how a retrieved source is represented in an AI answer. It does not establish that the same edits will make a brand more likely to appear in organic ChatGPT or Google AI search results.

Source: Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024. Paper and publication details

2. Competitive GEO in AI Answer Engines (May 2026)
Large controlled experiment

Finding: Topical relevance and the position of a source in the provided context were the strongest drivers of being cited first. Explicit pricing and recent timestamps also helped consistently. 
arXiv
+1

The researchers ran 252,000 trials across six LLMs, using paired comparisons in which candidate documents differed by one of 18 content factors. Brand anonymization and counterbalanced source order helped isolate the effects of content from brand and position biases.

Why it matters: This is unusually strong evidence for specific citation-selection mechanisms. It also suggests that superficial formatting changes alone are unlikely to be a reliable GEO strategy.

Limitation: The researchers supplied the competing documents directly to the model. The study primarily measures citation selection after retrieval, not whether publishing a page causes an AI search engine to discover it.

Source: What Gets Cited: Competitive GEO in AI Answer Engines — preprint.

3. What Drives AI Citations? (October 2026)
Recent empirical research

Finding: Pages whose titles and paragraphs match users’ questions are cited more often. The authors also found that retrieval behavior explains substantially more variation than page-level characteristics alone. 
arXiv

The study examined two 90-day collections covering 158,099 answers and 468,037 citations across commercial queries. It combined observational analysis with API-based replay experiments.

Its central insight is that citation visibility depends heavily on which pages the engine retrieves for its own reformulated queries. Brand familiarity also matters: models sometimes name brands without a supporting page being cited.

Why it matters: It separates two different optimization problems: getting a page retrieved and getting a brand mentioned. Improving on-page content may help with the first, but cannot guarantee the second.

Source: What Drives AI Citations? Content, Retrieval and Model Knowledge in GEO — preprint.

4. SafeGEO — AI product recommendations (2026)
Controlled recommendation study

Finding: GEO-style manipulations increased the rate at which targeted, flawed products entered recommendation sets by up to 83.2 percentage points in the tested scenarios.

The researchers constructed 600 recommendation cases and tested 22 manipulation variants.

Why it matters: It shows that content manipulation can affect actual recommendations, a more consequential outcome than citation frequency alone.

Limitation: This is a study of recommendation vulnerabilities under controlled conditions, including potentially misleading content. It does not establish that ordinary, truthful optimization will produce comparable gains in commercial AI search.

Source: SafeGEO: Understanding Generative Engine Optimization Risks in Recommendation Agents — preprint.

4. Why the evidence is harder to interpret than it looks

Three methodological issues explain why many GEO success stories overstate what their data proves.

1. Retrieval versus generation. If a page is never retrieved, improving its formatting may have no effect. A test that gives a page directly to an LLM isolates generation behavior but does not measure organic discoverability.

2. Stochastic outputs. The same question can yield different answers, citations, and recommendations across runs. A study published in April 2026 specifically recommends repeated measurements rather than judging visibility from a single response. 
arXiv

3. Confounding and competition. A brand might gain visibility because it became more famous, earned more media coverage, or improved its traditional search presence. And even if one brand gains recommendations, competitors may lose them. Controlled recommendation research highlights this competitive effect. 
alphaXiv
+1

A particularly relevant recent finding comes from an Axios report published October 28, 2026, covering a Muck Rack and Generative Pulse study. Across repeated ChatGPT queries on the same day, only 41% of cited domains overlapped. This illustrates why single-run comparisons are unreliable.

5. How to test GEO credibly yourself

For an independent builder testing whether a tactic improves a brand's AI visibility [personal context omitted], I would use the following protocol.

A small, defensible GEO experiment

Define one intervention. For example, publish a substantive third-party article about your work, or rewrite a page to answer a specific user question. Avoid changing several things at once.

Pre-register 20–50 relevant prompts. Include branded queries, non-branded discovery queries, and recommendation queries. Keep their wording fixed for the primary analysis.

Establish a baseline. Run each prompt multiple times across the selected AI engines and record brand mentions, recommendation inclusion, citations, and cited URLs.

Use controls. Compare treated pages or topics with similar untreated pages or topics. Where practical, stagger publication dates to help separate intervention effects from general time trends.

Measure over time. Repeat the observations for at least several weeks. Record engine version, date, location settings, and whether web search was enabled.

Report uncertainty. Calculate changes in mention and recommendation rates, not just raw counts. Include variation across runs, platforms, and prompt categories.

The primary metric should be the change in the proportion of eligible prompts that mention or recommend the brand, compared with the baseline and a suitable control. Citation rate should be reported separately.

Bottom line

The most defensible conclusions from the available evidence are:

Content relevance and source position can influence citation selection in controlled tests.

Brand mentions, third-party coverage, and YouTube presence correlate with AI visibility, but the causal effect of increasing these signals remains uncertain.

Recommendation manipulation can change model outputs, although that does not establish that legitimate marketing tactics will yield equivalent gains.

No single tactic has yet been shown convincingly to produce stable, sustained improvements in organic brand recommendations across major AI platforms.

For further reading, start with the Princeton GEO paper, then the competitive citation experiment and the October 2026 retrieval study. Together, they provide a stronger foundation than vendor playbooks based solely on correlation or isolated success stories.
