Original answer

Best free resources to learn GEO in 2026

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of improving the likelihood that AI-powered search and answer systems — such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews — discover, reference and recommend your content.

My recommendation is to learn GEO through three complementary approaches: official platform documentation, research with measurable evidence, and hands-on experiments on content you control. Free courses are useful for structure, but many GEO tactics are still evolving, so practical results matter more than certificates.

4. What the evidence says about GEO

Three principles are worth learning before spending time on optimization tactics.

Original research is more valuable than repeating popular advice. The foundational GEO paper introduced a systematic evaluation framework and reported visibility improvements of up to 40% under its experimental conditions. That result should not be treated as a guaranteed improvement for every site or today's AI systems. 
arXiv

Traditional SEO fundamentals remain important. Google's 2026 guidance emphasizes indexability, crawlability, useful original content and a clear technical structure. 
Google for Developers
+1

Be skeptical of universal GEO hacks. Google explicitly says its search systems do not require llms.txt, special GEO markup or artificially fragmented content. Other AI systems may behave differently, so test individual tactics against the platform you want to influence. 
Google for Developers

5. A practical four-week learning plan

I recommend a small experiment-driven curriculum. You can complete it without buying a GEO tool or running a website with significant traffic.

Week 1 — Understand the foundations

Read Google's guide and the original GEO paper.

Deliverable: a one-page summary distinguishing established evidence, platform-specific recommendations and unproven hypotheses.

Week 2 — Establish a baseline

Choose one topic you publish about regularly. Create 10–20 realistic questions your intended audience might ask, then test them on ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini.

Deliverable: a spreadsheet recording brand or author mentions, cited URLs, competitors mentioned and answer differences.

Week 3 — Run a controlled content experiment

Publish or improve one piece of content with original evidence, clear explanations and supporting sources. Keep a comparable page or topic unchanged where possible.

Deliverable: a documented hypothesis, before-and-after observations and limitations.

Week 4 — Analyze and publish your findings

Repeat the same questions under comparable conditions. Track mention rate, citation rate and the sources AI systems select. Where possible, supplement manual tests with webmaster analytics.

Deliverable: a short public case study explaining what changed, what did not, and what you would test next.

Manual tests are noisy because answers can vary with wording, model version and time. Treat early findings as exploratory; repeated observations and suitable comparison groups are needed before making causal claims.

My recommendation

Start with these three resources, in this order:

Google's official GEO guidance — establish the technical and content fundamentals.

The original GEO research paper — understand the evidence and experimental methodology.

LearnGEO — fill in the practical gaps with a structured course.

Then run a small experiment and publish the results. This will build both GEO knowledge and a portfolio of evidence-backed content.
